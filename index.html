The Open Pantry

What's in our kitchen — and what isn't.

We have nothing to hide. When you eat at Bell Kitchen, every plate is built from a short list of real ingredients we trust enough to name. We won't quietly use anything we wouldn't proudly put on our own table.

What we use

Sourced honestly · Named with pride

  • Spartan extra-virgin olive oil — From a producer of boutique olive oil.
  • Vegetables from the Athens central market — picked daily before service
  • Aegean fish from the Athens fish market when in season
  • Minced Meat from our Grinder Our restaurant uses a special meat grinder to ensure 100% control over the minced meat recipe in all our dishes.
  • Greek feta PDO — never imitation, never industrial
  • Bio Yogurt from small producers in Livadero and Mytilene.
  • Fermented bread — from a bakery dating back to 1907.
  • Flower of Salt - Rock Salt from the Kythira island
  • Wine from small Greek estates — a boutique winery in the mountain village of Arnados on Tinos
  • Fresh dough & béchamel made from scratch, every morning

What we don't use

Strictly never in our food

  • Industrial seed oils (sunflower, soybean, canola)
  • Flavour enhancers (MSG, artificial bouillon)
  • Palm oil, in any form
  • Frozen ready-made bases — no shortcuts, no boxes
  • Imitation feta or unnamed "white cheese"
  • Pre-mixed béchamel or powdered sauce bases
  • Mass-market tomato concentrate with additives
  • Colourings, preservatives, or artificial sweeteners
  • Anonymous bulk ingredients we can't trace to a person

Our Story

Same kitchen.
Same neighbourhood.
Almost seventy years.

The corner of Petmeza and Falirou has smelled of slow-cooked tomato since 1956 — when this room first opened as a working-class taverna for the families who built this part of Athens.

Today, under the Little Noe team, Cookaki continues the same idea with a lighter, more conscious hand: olive oil instead of butter, seasonal vegetables alongside the classics, and the same hospitality the neighbourhood has counted on for three generations.

We're proud to be the anti-tourist-trap that's somehow ended up minutes from the Acropolis Museum.

  • 1956 A working-class taverna opens on Petmeza 5. The first moussaka tray comes out of the wood-fired oven.
  • 1978 Three generations of Chefs welcome poets, students, and workers of post-war Koukaki.
  • 2009 The Acropolis Museum opens two blocks away. Locals worry the neighbourhood will lose its soul. The kitchen character doesn't change.
  • 2026 The Little Noe team takes over the legendary space — preserving the recipes, lightening the execution, honouring the address.

The Neighbourhood

Petmeza 5, Koukaki
— two blocks from the museum.

Koukaki is what the rest of Athens used to be: leafy, low-rise, full of bakeries that still wrap your bread in paper. We're in the middle of it — and a five-minute walk from the Acropolis Museum.

  • 5 min Walk to the Acropolis Museum
  • 10 min Walk to the Acropolis main entrance
  • 4 min Walk to Syngrou-Fix metro (Line 2)
  • 12 min Walk to Plaka & Monastiraki
  • 7 min Walk to Filopappou Hill viewpoint
  • 3 min Taxi from the Syngrou Avenue hotels — Grand Hyatt Athens & Athenaeum InterContinental

Cookaki is nestled in a strategic, relaxed spot at Petmeza 5, Koukaki — just a swift 3-minute taxi ride or a brief 10-minute stroll from major Syngrou Avenue landmarks, including the Grand Hyatt Athens and the Athenaeum InterContinental. Good to know

Questions travellers ask us

Is Cookaki near the Acropolis Museum?
Yes — Cookaki is at Petmeza 5 in Koukáki, about a five-minute walk from the Acropolis Museum and roughly ten minutes from the Acropolis entrance. The nearest metro is Syngrou-Fix (Line 2). See directions from the Acropolis →
Does Cookaki take reservations?
Yes. Walk-ins are welcome, but in high season (April–October) and at weekends we recommend booking. Reserve a table or WhatsApp +30 698 237 3505.
Is it authentic and local, or a tourist restaurant?
A genuine neighbourhood taverna cooking for Koukáki since 1956 — the address Athenians share with friends. Cookaki holds 4.8 ★ across 400+ Google reviews and 4.9 ★ on Tripadvisor.
Is Cookaki vegetarian-friendly?
Yes. There is a daily Garden Dish of the Day (€8.90), Greek salad, sfakianí pita with honey and seasonal vegetable mezédes; vegan options on request. See vegetarian Greek options →
What are the opening hours?
Monday–Saturday 09:00–16:00 and 19:00–22:00; Sunday 10:00–18:00. Staff speak Greek, English and French.
What should I order?
Start with the Traditional Moussaka (€9.90) or the daily fresh fish, try the langoustines with artisan linguine, and for something special ask for the Wild Fish & the Bell Nest. Finish with the San Sebastián burnt cheesecake.
How much does a meal cost?
Most mains are €8–€13; expect about €15–€20 per person for a full meal with a glass of wine — excellent value this close to the Acropolis.
